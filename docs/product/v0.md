# First Usable V0

**Status:** Approved direction

**Market:** GB / en-GB / GBP
**Audience:** Founder-first; tester-ready architecture

## Purpose

V0 proves a narrow shopping loop without pretending to prove the whole company thesis. It is scheduled by evidence gates rather than an arbitrary date.

## Required vertical slice

1. The shopper enters an arbitrary natural-language shopping request.
2. The system creates an independently identified, persisted `ShoppingTask`.
3. The system proposes and validates task-local decision criteria.
4. A deterministic, inspectable shopping brief appears.
5. The system chooses qualitatively between asking one question, searching, or showing/refining current results.
6. The shopper may answer or choose **Show me options now**.
7. Search hypotheses and purpose-labelled Google queries are generated from the current task revision.
8. A Google SERP provider returns real UK results through an internal adapter.
9. Results are normalised, conservatively deduplicated, and linked to their discovery query.
10. Available evidence produces task/run-contextual product observations.
11. Criterion assessments express coarse status plus qualitative relation, evidence, and uncertainty.
12. Results show honest reasons they may fit and important unknowns.
13. The shopper can Save, choose Not for me, undo, open a retailer, and refine naturally.
14. Refinement visibly updates state, makes prior judgement stale, and can trigger another search.
15. A deterministic current-decision summary turns the present assessments into
    one honest outcome: choose a meaningfully separated eligible leader, verify
    one decision-critical gap, acknowledge a real trade-off, declare a tie, or
    say that evidence/options are insufficient.
16. Saves, suppressions, current criteria, and latest results survive refresh.

Saved-product comparison is the next decision-UX layer. It should not delay proving the search → react → refine → search-again loop.

## Product behaviour

### Landing

A sharp consumer promise, one dominant natural-language input, and a few unrelated example requests. No category form or filter onboarding.

### Questioning

One question is presented at a time. There is no total question limit. Questions should plausibly change retrieval, eligibility, or judgement; expected impact and effort remain qualitative reasoning, never numeric scores. Existing products remain visible when a post-search question appears.

### Shopping brief

The visible brief is a deterministic projection of active explicit or user-confirmed criteria. It uses natural language such as “Around £30, flexible” or “Nike preferred, others welcome.” It never exposes search hypotheses, confidence scores, or ontology terms. Applied changes are visible and reversible.

### Results

Products dominate the interface. Cards show imagery, merchant, conservatively worded observed price, why the product may fit, a meaningful trade-off or evidence gap, and clear actions. Weak evidence never becomes a confident claim.

Above the detailed cards, the current-decision summary gives the shopper the
smallest justified conclusion from the current revision's evidence. It names a
leader only when criterion-level evidence meaningfully separates it from the
best eligible alternative. A hard conflict removes an option from recommendation
eligibility; an unresolved hard requirement prevents a ready-to-buy conclusion;
ties, weak evidence, and popularity-only leads remain explicit. The summary may
show one grounded alternative and one decision-changing research action, while
the cards and comparison retain the inspectable evidence underneath.

The initial visual hypothesis is a warm off-white canvas, near-black text, and one restrained deep-green accent. It is not an invariant: validate it through desktop/mobile screenshots and change it if another direction better achieves premium, calm, trustworthy consumer quality.

### Reactions and refinement

A bare rejection suppresses only the known candidate/listing within this task and supports undo. A voluntary explanation becomes a new user message processed by the standard state-update contract. Saves survive reruns. Refinement produces observable changes in the brief and subsequent search/judgement.

## First usable V0 definition of done

- Multiple independently identified tasks are supported in persistence, even if the UI exposes one active task.
- The current task has a monotonic revision and idempotent actions.
- GB market context is explicit rather than scattered through code.
- Unknown preferences are not manufactured.
- Flexible budgets, exclusions, preferences, comparative criteria, removal, and change of mind survive deterministic tests.
- Natural input can create or update the brief through a validated model proposal.
- One useful question can be asked and skipped without a fixed question count.
- Real Google-backed results are retrieved through an isolated provider.
- Every displayed candidate is traceable to a run and query.
- Observations, assessments, and candidate judgement remain separate.
- Product-facing claims reference evidence or state uncertainty.
- Save, rejection, undo, refinement, rerun, and refresh work without corrupting state.
- Partial external failure preserves authoritative state and produces a recoverable experience.
- The cap, shelving, and headphones cases pass the relevant layer-specific evals.
- Desktop and mobile browser screenshots have been reviewed for consumer quality.

## Non-goals

- Public beta or full authentication
- Workspaces or long-term memory
- Multi-market UI
- Multiple search engines
- Checkout or affiliates
- Broad page crawling
- Long-term product knowledge reuse
- Cross-retailer fuzzy product identity
- Behavioural preference learning
- Universal ontology
- Vector database, fine-tuning, microservices, or queues
- Room visualisation
- Numeric match scoring

## Later quality layers

- Lightweight comparison of two or three saved products
- Selective product-page evidence acquisition if SERP evidence proves insufficient
- Result-aware visual questions and “more like this” shortcuts
- Stronger product identity and price/offer comparison
- Invited-user authentication and privacy hardening
- Long-term/workspace context with explicit promotion rules
- For future room contexts, one final visualisation after selection and measurement verification—not an image for every candidate
